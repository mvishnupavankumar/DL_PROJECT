AegisOps controlled research experiment
=======================================

Target: 40 fault episodes
Fault classes: 20 latency; 20 HTTP-error
Controls: Healthy-only trials recorded separately

Primary evaluation:
1. Detection performance by detector and fault class.
2. Detection latency from injection to first alert.
3. False alarms during verified healthy periods.
4. Root-cause diagnosis accuracy against the known injected fault.
5. Recovery success based on independent post-action verification.
6. Time to verified recovery, plus failed or incomplete trials.

Experimental safeguards:
- Do not use individual telemetry samples as independent fault episodes.
- Do not count an approval or action-completed message as proof of recovery.
- Record raw model, rule-based, and combined decisions separately.
- Record every eligible trial, including missed detections and failed recovery.
- Record diagnosis before revealing the known fault label to the evaluator where practical.
- Use explicitly approved local-demo recovery only.
- Do not overwrite the existing database or model checkpoint.
- Preserve timestamps in UTC and document any missing measurements.

Important:
This file is an initial protocol outline, not a completed experiment.
Freeze thresholds and comparison procedures before the evaluation run.


Trial procedure and scoring rules
---------------------------------
1. Freeze and record the model checkpoint, model threshold, rule thresholds,
   application version, configuration, and comparison procedures before trials.
   Do not tune them using evaluation-trial outcomes.

2. Use one fault episode as the unit of analysis. Each episode begins from a
   verified healthy service state. Record a UTC baseline timestamp, inject one
   fault, observe for a predeclared fixed observation window, reset the fault,
   and independently verify recovery. Record the exact observation-window
   duration before starting formal trials.

3. Randomize the order of the 20 latency and 20 HTTP-error episodes where
   practical. Record the randomization procedure and seed. Do not exclude
   episodes because a detector misses the fault or recovery fails.

4. Record raw model predictions, rule-based predictions, and combined alerts
   separately. Preserve score, threshold, detector mode, timestamps, and
   relevant telemetry. A missing alert by the end of the observation window
   is a missed detection, not a missing trial.

5. Evaluate detection against the injected fault episode, not individual
   correlated telemetry rows. Report episode-level detection rate by fault
   class, false-positive rate during verified healthy controls, precision and
   recall where labels support them, and detection latency. Define the alert
   onset rule before the evaluation; do not choose it after seeing results.

6. Evaluate diagnosis against the injected fault class: latency or HTTP error.
   Capture the baseline and agent-assisted diagnoses before revealing the
   injected label to the evaluator where practical. Use the same evidence
   availability and scoring rubric across compared workflows. Record
   unclassifiable and missing diagnoses explicitly.

7. Recovery is successful only when independent post-action checks confirm
   the service is healthy, the fault mode is normal, the probe returns HTTP
   200, and latency is below the predeclared limit of 1000 ms. Record each
   check, recovery attempt, and verification timestamp. Approval or an action
   completion message alone is insufficient evidence.

8. Report time to verified recovery from fault injection to the first
   successful independent verification. Report unsuccessful or unverified
   recoveries separately; do not calculate successful-recovery time for them
   as though they succeeded.

9. Healthy controls must use the same observation duration and alert criteria
   as specified in the protocol. Verify normal service state during the
   control period and record any alerts. Determine the number and scheduling
   of healthy controls before the evaluation.

10. Preserve raw records and UTC timestamps. Document missing data, service
    interruptions, deviations, and exclusions with reasons. Never silently
    remove failed, incomplete, or inconvenient trials.

11. Report results by fault class and comparison workflow, with uncertainty
    appropriate to the number of independent episodes. Forty episodes are a
    pilot-scale evaluation; avoid broad claims of generalization from this
    single local demo service.

Pre-trial readiness gate
------------------------
Formal trials must not begin until:
- The observation-window duration and healthy-control count are specified.
- The detector, rules, diagnosis workflows, and scoring rubric are frozen.
- Raw model, rule, and combined outputs can be captured for every trial.
- A trial recorder preserves injection, alert, reset, and verification times.
- A healthy baseline has been checked and the existing high alert rate has
  been investigated and documented.
- The local service is bound only to 127.0.0.1 and recovery remains explicitly
  approved and local-only.

Current status
